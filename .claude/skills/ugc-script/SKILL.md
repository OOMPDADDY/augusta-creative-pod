# UGC Script

## The output

A two column script. Spoken on the left, on screen on the right.

## The prompt

```
Below is the argument brief, the chosen hook, the shot sheet and the customer
phrase bank.

BRIEF: [PASTE]
HOOK: [PASTE]
PHRASE BANK: [PASTE]

Write the script.

Emit only a two column table:

| Spoken | On screen |

Rules:
- Opens on the hook. The product is not named in the first five seconds.
- Uses the customer's own phrases from the bank wherever they fit. Do not
  smooth them into marketing language.
- Carries the proof as a demonstration, not a statistic read aloud.
- Handles the one objection from the brief, in one sentence, in passing.
- Under 120 spoken words. Count them and state the count on a final line.
- No line may contain a word the creator would not say to a friend.
- Never use the pattern "Not X. Y."
```

## The five second rule

Naming the brand in the first five seconds is the most reliable way to make a
UGC ad look like an ad, and the moment it looks like an ad the viewer applies ad
defenses to everything that follows. The claim lands harder when the product
arrives as the answer to something already established.

## What to do with it

To the creator with the shot sheet. `talent-brief` carries the delivery notes
that do not belong in the script itself, because a creator reading stage
directions performs them.
