# Talent Brief

## The output

A one page creator brief. Eight sections, plain language.

## The prompt

```
Below is the argument brief, the script and the shot sheet.

SOURCE: [PASTE]

Write the creator brief. Exactly these sections:

THE ONE THING     what this video has to make a viewer believe, one sentence
DELIVERY          pace, energy and tone, described by comparison to a real
                  situation rather than by adjective
SETTING           where to film, and two alternatives if that is not available
WARDROBE          what to wear and what to avoid, and why
TECHNICAL         orientation, resolution, audio, lighting, framing minimums
THE PROOF SHOT    the mandatory shot, described so it cannot be misread
DO NOT            the specific things that make footage unusable here
DELIVERABLES      what to send back, in what format, by when

Write it to a creator, not to a marketer. No jargon. No brand vocabulary.
```

## Why DO NOT is its own section

Creators are given aspirational briefs and then judged against unstated
constraints. Naming the failure modes explicitly, filming in portrait when you
need square safe areas, holding the product so the label faces away, cutting the
hook short, removes almost every avoidable reshoot in one paragraph.

## What to do with it

Send with the script and the shot sheet as one package. Anything the creator
asks about after receiving all three is a genuine gap in this brief, so fold the
answer back in before the next one goes out.
