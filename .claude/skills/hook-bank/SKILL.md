# Hook Bank

## The output

Twenty hooks for one argument. Line and shot, paired.

## The prompt

```
Below is one argument brief and the purchase triggers.

BRIEF: [PASTE]
TRIGGERS: [PASTE]

Write 20 hooks. A hook is the first three seconds: what is said and what is
shown, together.

Emit only:

| # | Type | Spoken line | What is on screen | Words | Trigger used |

Types, five of each:
  SITUATION   opens inside the trigger moment
  CLAIM       opens on the argument stated flat
  QUESTION    opens on the buyer's own unresolved question
  DEMO        opens on the product doing the thing, no words for one beat

Rules:
- Spoken line is 12 words or fewer. Count them.
- What is on screen must be filmable with one person and the product.
- No hook may open on a logo, a brand name, or the words "introducing" or
  "meet".
- Every hook makes the same argument as the brief.
```

## Why the line and the shot are one field

Hooks written as copy get handed to an editor who picks whatever footage exists,
and the visual ends up illustrating the line rather than carrying it. The best
hooks are usually the ones where the shot does the work and the line is almost
redundant, and you cannot find those by writing lines alone.

## What to do with it

The top five go to `shot-sheet` and `ugc-script`. Keep the rest. When an
argument wins and needs iteration, the hook bank is where the next five come
from, and no new research is required.
